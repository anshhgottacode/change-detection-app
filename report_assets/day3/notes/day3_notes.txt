## 2. Alignment settings (pipeline.py defaults)

- ORB features: 3000
- Ratio-test threshold: 0.75
- RANSAC reprojection threshold: 4.0 px
- Minimum inliers: 15
- Homography sanity check: determinant of the 2x2 part between 0.5 and 2.0
- Gaussian blur: 5x5; maximum width: 1280 px; Otsu threshold; morphology 5x5; minimum region area 200 px
- CLAHE: clip limit 2.0, 8x8 tiles. Histogram matching: skimage match_histograms to the before-image.
- On alignment, black warp borders are excluded through a "valid" mask. The Otsu threshold and the change percentage are computed over valid pixels only, so the denominator of aligned variants is smaller than that of unaligned variants.
- No manual changes to these settings were recorded for this run.

## 4. Border fix (important for the paper)

Problem found: in the first Day 3 code, illumination normalisation was applied to the aligned after-image while it still had a black warp border, so CLAHE and histogram matching treated the black pixels as image content.
Fix: the warp border is filled with the before-image's pixels before preprocessing.

Measured effect of the fix on variants that use normalisation after alignment:

| Pair | Variant | Before fix | After fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| pair3 | V7 align + match | 28.656% | 3.019% |
| shiftstatic | V7 align + match | 11.527% | 7.443% |
| nochange | V7 align + match | 2.486% | 4.091% |
| nochange | V6 align + CLAHE | 9.465% | 11.534% |

Small changes also appeared in variants without normalisation (for example pair3 V3: 2.628% to 2.806%). Interpretation (not verified): the fill changes pixels next to the edge of the valid region, which slightly alters the blurred values there. The results in this file are from the corrected code only.

## 6. False-change rates and observations

Camera shift (Measured):
- shiftstatic: diff 25.927% to 4.595% with alignment (V1 vs V3).
- pair3: diff 30.219% to 2.806% with alignment. The raw 50/50 blend of pair3 shows ghosting (double images), so the camera moved between its photos; alignment removed most of the apparent change. TODO: state what pair3 shows and whether it also contains real changes.
- SSIM also dropped with alignment: shiftstatic 74.101% to 28.783%, pair3 56.368% to 17.689%, nochange 13.810% to 1.048%. On shiftstatic and pair3 it stayed much higher than diff + align.
- On pairs where the camera did not move, alignment changed little: pair1 1.846% to 1.855%, lightstatic 43.853% to 43.750%. Interpretation (not verified): the estimated warp is then close to the identity.

Lighting (Measured, lightstatic):
- Diff alone 43.853%; CLAHE 19.432%; histogram matching 22.279%.
- SSIM without any normalisation gave 13.700% and SSIM + align 13.672%, the lowest values on this pair.
- Alignment did not help here (43.853% to 43.750%), as expected for a fixed camera.

Normalisation after alignment (Measured): on shiftstatic and pair3, adding CLAHE or matching to alignment did not beat alignment alone (shiftstatic: 4.595 alone vs 7.736 CLAHE and 7.443 match; pair3: 2.806 alone vs 3.473 and 3.019).

No single variant was best on every static pair. Diff alone was lowest on nochange (0.759%), diff + align on shiftstatic (4.595%), and SSIM variants on lightstatic (about 13.7%).

## 7. Real-change check on pair1 (Measured)

Normalisation effect (V1 vs V4 / V5): 1.846% to 2.981% with CLAHE (+1.135 points) and to 1.953% with histogram matching (+0.107 points). Alignment alone (V1 vs V3) gave 1.846% to 1.855%.
The change percentage stayed in a similar range. This does not show that the same regions were detected; that needs ground-truth masks (Day 6). (The earlier version of these notes compared V1 with V3, which measures alignment, not normalisation.)

## 8. Unexpected result: nochange got worse with alignment

Measured: nochange diff 0.759% (V1) rises to 16.973% with alignment (V3), with 11.534% for V6 and 4.091% for V7. The Otsu threshold was 9 for V3 against 26 for V1. SSIM + align was 1.048%. The aligned blend (nochange\diff_align\8_blend_aligned.png) shows slight ghosting.

Interpretation (not verified): alignment may introduce small misregistration or resampling blur; a homography models only a plane or a pure camera rotation, so a 3D scene with parallax cannot be aligned perfectly. The very low Otsu threshold (9) may also inflate the percentage, since percentages at different thresholds are not directly comparable.

Updated Section 8 — Fixed-threshold check
Measured: nochange diff was 0.759% (V1) with Otsu thresholding and rose to 16.973% with alignment (V3). The fixed-threshold check at threshold 30 gave:
Pair	Alignment	Change %	Regions	Time
nochange	Off	0.48%	4	4 ms
nochange	On	0.00%	0	1864 ms
shiftstatic	Off	70.82%	18	8 ms
shiftstatic	On	15.22%	34	783 ms
Alignment succeeded in both aligned runs. For nochange, alignment reported 1,314 good matches and 1,236 inliers. For shiftstatic, it reported 296 good matches and 186 inliers.

Fixed-threshold visual inspection: At threshold 30, alignment reduced shiftstatic change from 70.82% (18 regions) to 15.22% (34 regions). The aligned mask contains scattered detections across the scene, not solely along the borders. This suggests that residual differences remain after alignment; their precise cause is unconfirmed.
Interpretation (not verified): At the same threshold of 30, alignment reduced the detected change percentage from 0.48% to 0.00% on nochange, and from 70.82% to 15.22% on shiftstatic. This supports the idea that alignment reduces apparent change caused by camera movement. However, shiftstatic still has 34 detected regions, so residual differences remain. These fixed-threshold results should be considered separately from the earlier Otsu-threshold ablation results.

## 11. Overall conclusion (supported only)

Alignment strongly reduced the change measured for the two pairs in which the camera moved (shiftstatic 25.927% to 4.595%, pair3 30.219% to 2.806%) and had little effect where it did not. CLAHE and histogram matching reduced the false change on the lighting pair (43.853% to 19.432% and 22.279%), though SSIM alone gave the lowest value there. On the no-change pair, alignment with differencing increased the apparent change (0.759% to 16.973%), which is an unresolved limitation. Pair2 could not be aligned. None of these results measure accuracy; precision, recall, F1 and IoU need ground truth.
